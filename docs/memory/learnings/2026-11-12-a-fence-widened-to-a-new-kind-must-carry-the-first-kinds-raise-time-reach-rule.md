## 2026-11-12 — pitfall — a fence widened to a new address kind must carry the first kind's raise-time preconditions with it
**Context**: #4501. The answer to an ask addressed to a canon role (`role:<id>`) was fenced to the people recorded at the raise, and that raise already kept only the people who can open the agent (`role_cannot_open_agent` when none can). The same fence was then extended to the `approver` kind by adding one clause to the ending predicate (`may_end`) — but the `approver` raise kept the plain resolution, with no reach check. A seated approver the agent was not shared with was recorded as the only person who may answer an ask they could never see: `201 resolved: true`, a 404 for them, `403 not_addressee` for everyone else. Before the fence the same row was survivable, because anyone with access could answer it.
**Lesson**: A fence ("only the recorded audience may end this") is safe only together with the rule that decides who gets recorded. When widening a fence to another kind, diff the two raise paths, not just the ending predicate: every precondition the first kind's raise applies (reach, non-empty audience, one reader) must hold for the new kind, or the fence turns a mis-addressed row into an undecidable one. Put the filter in the shared resolver (`role_addressing`) so the kinds cannot drift, and pin it with a test that seats a person who cannot open the agent — a fixture where "everyone can open every agent" passes with the check missing.
